# Generated, not computed

<!-- index: 9#1, 9#2; 4#3 attributed -->

This lesson is about what ChatGPT gets wrong, and it begins with what
ChatGPT is doing when it answers, because the failures described in the
sections that follow are easier to recognise once the vendor's own
description of the tool is in view.

OpenAI's help-center page on whether ChatGPT tells the truth says that
ChatGPT is designed to provide useful responses based on patterns in the
data it was trained on, and that, like any language model, it can produce
incorrect or misleading outputs. The two halves of that statement belong
together: the response is drawn from patterns, and the possibility of a
wrong response is described by the vendor as something true of any
language model. The same page adds that the response may sound confident
even when it is wrong.

## What "patterns" does and does not tell you

That is as far as the vendor's description goes. It does not say how the
patterns are found or how a response is assembled from them, and no other
source this course relies on explains it either. This course therefore does
not explain it; a participant who wants an account of the mechanism should
look for one elsewhere and treat this lesson as silent on it. What the
lesson takes from the description is the one word that carries the weight:
*pattern*. A response is produced from patterns in training data. That is
the vendor's account of where a response comes from, and it is the whole of
what this course claims about it.

## Generated, not computed

The CPA.com Generative AI Toolkit, a 2023 introduction to generative AI
written for accounting and finance professionals, quotes Jeff Seibert,
chief executive of Digits, as saying that today's large language models
"produce generated output, not computed answers," and that this means you
should not trust them with math or financial analysis. That is one
executive's phrasing, quoted in a trade body's toolkit; it is not a finding
of the toolkit, of CPA.com, or of this course, and it is reported here as a
quotation. It is reported at all because it names the distinction this
section is built on more cleanly than any other sentence in the sources: a
generated response and a computed answer are two different things. The
sections that follow are about what happens when the first is mistaken for
the second.

Section 05 returns to the distinction, because there is one case in which a
number from ChatGPT is computed rather than generated: when the model uses
a tool built for that purpose. Until then, the lesson's working assumption
is the vendor's own: the output may be incorrect or misleading, and it may
not sound it.
