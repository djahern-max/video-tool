# Confidence is not correctness

<!-- index: 9#2, 9#6, 9#7; 4#1 attributed -->

OpenAI's page states the limitation in three words — "Confidence isn't
reliability" — and explains it: the model may express high confidence even
in incorrect answers. The same page has already said, in its opening, that
ChatGPT might sound confident even when it is wrong. The CPA.com toolkit
says the same from the reader's side: generative AI's confident,
articulate and well-reasoned answers may mask the failing of getting
things wrong and making things up.

In ordinary professional life, tone carries information: a colleague who
hedges is telling you something, and one who does not is telling you
something else. The sources say that the same inference does not hold
here: the confidence expressed in a response is not evidence about whether
the response is correct.

The three failures this lesson has described so far — hallucination,
fabricated citation, and staleness — have this in common: on the sources'
account, none of them announces itself in the tone of the response.
OpenAI's own conclusion from that is to encourage users to approach ChatGPT
critically and verify important information from reliable sources. The
check has to come from outside the response, because nothing inside it —
least of all how sure it sounds — can supply it. That is lesson 4's
subject.
