# Gain Contingencies Wait

The rules are deliberately asymmetric. A gain contingency — the same
lawsuit seen from the plaintiff's side — is **not** reflected in the
financial statements before it is realized, because to do so would
recognize revenue before realization. ASC 450-30-25-1 carries that
principle forward from practice long predating the Codification:
probability is not enough; a probable win is still only a contingency
until it is resolved.

Disclosure of a gain contingency is permitted, but ASC 450-30-50-1
requires care that the disclosure avoids **misleading implications** about
the likelihood of realization — a note that reads like a promised
recovery defeats the purpose of not recognizing one.

The asymmetry is conservatism doing exactly what it is meant to do:
probable losses are accrued on estimate, probable gains wait for the
cash, the judgment, or the signed settlement. When you see a receivable
recorded for an unresolved claim, that is the red flag this section
exists to make you notice.
