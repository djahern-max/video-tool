# Measuring Within a Range

Often the loss cannot be pinned to a single number — counsel says the case
will probably settle somewhere between $2 million and $9 million. ASC
450-20-30-1 answers this directly: when the reasonable estimate of the
loss is a range, the "reasonably estimated" condition is met, and an
amount is accrued.

Which amount depends on the shape of the range:

- If some amount **within the range** appears to be a **better estimate**
  than any other amount in it, that amount is accrued.
- If **no amount** in the range is a better estimate than any other, the
  **minimum** of the range is accrued.

So the $2 million to $9 million case with no better point estimate accrues
$2 million — not the midpoint, not an expected value, and never zero. The
minimum is the floor the standard permits, on the reasoning that at least
that much loss is probable. The rest of the exposure does not vanish: the
$7 million of additional reasonably possible loss above the accrual is
exactly what the disclosure requirements in the next section exist to
communicate.
