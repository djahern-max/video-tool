# Clear, specific, and in context

<!-- index: 13#3 (and the sentence that follows it on the page, J1) -->

The first of OpenAI's practices is about the request itself. The page
says to ensure your prompts are clear, specific, and provide enough
context for the model to understand what you are asking, and it adds:
avoid ambiguity and be as precise as possible to get accurate and
relevant responses. That is one sentence of advice with three parts, and
a prompt can meet one part and miss the others.

## Clear

Clear is about the words. The page's own word for the fault is
*ambiguity*: a request that can be read more than one way is one the page
says to fix before it is sent, not after the response shows which way it
was read. "Tell me about leases" can be read as a request for a
definition, a history, a comparison, or a list, and nothing in it says
which.

## Specific

Specific is about the ask. "Summarise this" and "Summarise this in five
sentences for a reader who has not seen the underlying document" are both
clear; only the second is specific. The page's word is *precise*, and it
ties precision to what comes back: as precise as possible, to get
accurate and relevant responses.

## Context

Context is what the model needs in order to understand what you are
asking, in the page's phrase. A prompt that names the reader, the
purpose, and the material the model is to work from carries context; one
that names only the task does not. The test the page states is the
model's, not a colleague's: enough context *for the model to understand
what you are asking*. A request a colleague would understand may still
fall short of it, because the colleague knows the client, the engagement,
and the firm's house style, and the model knows none of that unless the
prompt says it.

## What this practice does and does not do

The page does not say that a clearer prompt produces a truer answer, and
this lesson does not say so either. What the three parts do is narrow
what the model is being asked for, so that the response is an answer to
the question actually meant. Whether the answer is correct is a separate
matter, and lesson 4 is about checking it.
