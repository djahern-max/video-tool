# What Must Be Disclosed

Accrual and disclosure are separate obligations, and the disclosure
requirements catch what the accrual test lets through. Under ASC
450-20-50-3 and 50-4, disclosure of a loss contingency is required when
there is **at least a reasonable possibility** that a loss (or a loss in
excess of any amount already accrued) may have been incurred, and either
no accrual was made — because one of the two recognition conditions
failed — or the exposure exceeds the amount accrued.

The disclosure itself has two parts:

- the **nature** of the contingency; and
- an **estimate of the possible loss or range of loss**, or a statement
  that such an estimate cannot be made.

That second bullet is not optional silence: if no estimate can be made,
the notes must say so. For the range example from the previous section,
the company accrues $2 million and discloses the additional exposure up
to $9 million. Remote contingencies generally require no disclosure,
with narrow exceptions (guarantees being the familiar one) addressed
elsewhere in the Codification.
