# When a Loss Is Accrued

ASC 450-20-25-2 sets two conditions, and an estimated loss from a loss
contingency is accrued by a charge to income only when **both** are met:

1. Information available before the financial statements are issued (or
   are available to be issued) indicates that it is **probable** that an
   asset had been impaired or a liability had been incurred **at the date
   of the financial statements**; and
2. The amount of loss can be **reasonably estimated**.

Three things practitioners miss. First, the assessment is made with
everything known before issuance, but the condition being assessed is the
state of affairs at the balance sheet date — a loss event that happens
after year end is a different animal, handled by the subsequent-events
guidance, not accrued here. Second, the conditions are conjunctive: a
probable loss that cannot be reasonably estimated is not accrued, and a
precisely estimable loss that is merely reasonably possible is not
accrued either. Third, failing the accrual test does not end the
analysis — it moves the contingency into the disclosure regime covered
later in this guide.
