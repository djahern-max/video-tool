# Tone and examples

<!-- index: 13#5; 4#9 -->

Two more things a prompt can carry: a tone, and an example of what a good
answer looks like. The first is OpenAI's practice; the second is the
CPA.com toolkit's.

## Tone

OpenAI's page says to use descriptive adjectives to indicate the tone,
and it lists the kind of words that help guide the model: formal,
informal, friendly, professional, humorous, or serious. The practice is
a word or two, placed in the request. A draft that will go to a client
can be asked for as formal or as professional in the same sentence that
asks for it; a note to a colleague can be asked for as informal. The tone
is named in the prompt rather than corrected in the response, which is
section 04's subject.

## Examples

The CPA.com toolkit says that providing the model with examples helps get
a better output. An example does for the shape of the answer what an
adjective does for its tone: it shows what is wanted instead of
describing it. What an example looks like depends on the task. For a
letter, an example is a letter of the same kind; for a table, it is a row
filled in by hand; for a list of points, it is one point written the way
the rest should be. What may be put into an example is lesson 5's
subject, and section 05 gives the one thing the toolkit says about it.

## The two together

A prompt that names its tone and shows an example has told the model two
things the task alone does not: how the answer should sound, and what it
should look like. Neither makes the answer correct. Both make it closer
to usable on the first try, which is the difference between a response
that is refined and one that is discarded.
