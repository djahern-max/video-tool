# The Likelihood Scale

A contingency is an existing condition, situation, or set of circumstances
involving uncertainty as to possible gain or loss that will ultimately be
resolved when one or more future events occur or fail to occur. A pending
lawsuit is the classic example: the condition exists today, and only a
future event — the verdict or the settlement — resolves it.

Everything in ASC 450 turns on how likely that confirming future event is,
and the standard gives the scale exactly three points:

- **Probable** — the future event or events are likely to occur.
- **Reasonably possible** — the chance of the future event or events
  occurring is more than remote but less than likely.
- **Remote** — the chance of the future event or events occurring is
  slight.

These are defined terms, not general-usage words. "Probable" does not mean
"more likely than not" — it sits higher on the scale than that, at
*likely*. Every recognition and disclosure decision in the rest of this
course starts by placing the contingency on this scale, so the placement
deserves real deliberation, not a reflex.
