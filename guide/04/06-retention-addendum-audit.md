# Retention, the addendum, and the audit

<!-- index: 10#8, 10#14, 10#15, 10#19, 10#20; 11#6 (glossary only) -->

Three more things the enterprise privacy page commits to for a workspace,
each of which a firm's policy can lean on.

## Retention

On ChatGPT Enterprise, the page says, workspace admins control how long
data is retained, and any deleted conversations are removed from OpenAI's
systems within 30 days unless OpenAI is legally required to retain them.
On ChatGPT Business, workspace admins can likewise control how long data
is retained; any deleted or unsaved conversations are removed within 30
days, unless longer retention is required by law or is reasonably
necessary to protect OpenAI's services or any third party from harm. The
two answers are close but not identical: the Business answer adds unsaved
conversations, and it adds harm prevention as a ground for keeping them
longer. Retention, in other words, is a setting the firm's admin holds,
not a default the firm inherits. What the firm should set it to is a
policy question, and lesson 5's.

## The Data Processing Addendum

The page says OpenAI is able to execute a Data Processing Addendum with
customers for their use of ChatGPT Business, ChatGPT Enterprise, and the
API. It is the one item in this section that takes the form of an
agreement the firm and OpenAI execute. Lesson 5 takes up what a CPA needs
from a service provider in writing.

## SOC 2 Type 2

The page says that ChatGPT Enterprise, ChatGPT Edu, and ChatGPT for
Healthcare have each successfully completed a SOC 2 Type 2 audit, and,
separately, that ChatGPT Business has successfully completed a SOC 2
Type 2 audit. Both plans, then, carry the same statement. What such an
audit examines is a subject of its own; the glossary gives the page's
one-line description of what OpenAI says its SOC 2 audit confirms, and
lesson 5 says what a CPA can do with a report of that kind.

## What the workspace adds, in one list

Set against section 02's single switch, the enterprise privacy page gives
a workspace four things: admin access to members' conversations, admin
control of retention, a Data Processing Addendum, and a SOC 2 Type 2
audit. Each is a statement about the plan, not about the firm. It says
what OpenAI offers; lesson 5 is about what the firm then has to do.
