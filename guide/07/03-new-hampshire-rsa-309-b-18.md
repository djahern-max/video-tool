# State law: New Hampshire RSA 309-B:18

<!-- index: 1#13, 5#11, 5#10, 5#2, 5#3, 5#1, 5#4, 5#5, 5#6, 5#7, 5#8, 5#9 -->

The Code's interpretation on third-party providers ends with a direction
outward. It says the member should consider whether federal, state, or
local statutes, rules, or regulations concerning the confidentiality of
client information may be more restrictive than the requirements in the
interpretation. This section takes one state statute as its example:
section 309-B:18 of the New Hampshire Accountancy Act, RSA chapter
309-B. No other state's law is stated in this course.

## What the statute says

The section dates from 1999 and was last amended effective July 1, 2024.
Its duty is stated in one sentence with the exception at its head.
Except by permission of the client for whom a licensee performs
services, or the heirs, successors, or personal representatives of such
client, a licensee or any partner, officer, member, manager,
shareholder, or employee of a licensee shall not voluntarily disclose
information communicated to such person by the client relating to and in
connection with services rendered to the client by the licensee. The
statute then adds that such information shall be deemed confidential.
Confidential by statute, that is, and not by any agreement between the
licensee and the client.

## Who it binds and what it covers

Three features of the sentence are the ones this lesson draws out.
First, the duty binds everyone in the licensee's firm, not only the
licensee: partners, officers, members, managers, shareholders and
employees are named. Second, what is protected is information
communicated by the client relating to and in connection with the
services rendered. Third, the release at the head of the sentence is the
permission of the client, or of the client's heirs, successors or
personal representatives, and the statute does not say that the
permission must be written, specific, or informed. The Code's "specific
consent", with its three required contents, has no counterpart in the
section's text.

## The exceptions

The rest of the section lists what the chapter is not to be read as
prohibiting. Nothing in the chapter prohibits the disclosure of
information required to be disclosed by the standards of the public
accounting profession in reporting on the examination of financial
statements. Nothing prohibits disclosures in court proceedings or
administrative proceedings before governmental agencies where a subpoena
or summons has been issued. Nothing prohibits disclosure in
investigations or proceedings under RSA 310, in ethical investigations
conducted by private professional organizations, or in the course of
peer reviews. And nothing prohibits disclosure to other persons active
in the organization performing services for that client on a need to
know basis, or to persons in such professional organization, peer review
entity, or organization performing services for that client who need
the information for the sole purpose of assuring quality control.

## What is not on the list

There is no service-provider clause. The Code's first route, a
confidentiality contract with reasonable assurance and no client
consent, has no counterpart in the section: its exceptions name
reporting standards, legal process, investigations and peer review,
need-to-know sharing inside the organization serving the client, and
quality control, and its one general release is the client's
permission. Whether a vendor operating software falls within "persons in
such professional organization, peer review entity, or organization
performing services for that client" is not something the section
addresses, and this course does not say that it does. On the section's
text, then, the state rule is narrower than the Code's interpretation,
and client permission is its only general release. The section never
names technology, a vendor, or a computer system of any kind, and what
"voluntarily disclose" means is not defined in it. Section 04 says what
this course makes of that.
