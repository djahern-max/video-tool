# The Confidential Client Information Rule

<!-- index: 1#1, 1#2, 1#3, 1#4, 1#5 -->

Lesson 2 was about what OpenAI does with what goes into ChatGPT. This
lesson is about what a CPA's own rules say may go in at all, and it
begins with the rule of the AICPA Code of Professional Conduct that the
question turns on.

## The Rule

The Confidential Client Information Rule, in section 1.700.001 of the
Code, is one sentence: a member in public practice shall not disclose
any confidential client information without the specific consent of the
client. Those are the Rule's words. Everything else in this lesson's
account of the Code is about what the words mean and what the Code's
interpretations add to them.

## What is confidential

The Code's definitions give the term its meaning: confidential client
information is any information obtained from the client that is not
available to the public. The definition has two parts, and both matter.
The information has to have come from the client, and it has to be
unavailable to the public. The Code's definitions also say what
"available to the public" includes: information on publicly accessible
websites, databases, online discussion forums, or other electronic media
by which members of the public can access the information. Information
of that kind is outside the Rule.

## The default

The Code's interpretations set the direction of doubt: unless the
particular client information is available to the public, such
information should be considered confidential client information. The
starting point is therefore confidential, and the question in any given
case is whether the information is public, not whether it is sensitive.

## A client's name

The Code gives one example of how far the term reaches. In its
discussion of the Rule, it says that if a member's practice is limited
to bankruptcy matters, disclosure of the client's name could suggest
that the client may be experiencing financial difficulties, which may be
confidential client information. The name is not confidential because
names are secret; it is confidential because, in that practice,
disclosing it tells a stranger something about the client that the
client did not make public. The example is the Code's, and its point is
that the test is what a disclosure reveals, not what kind of fact is
disclosed.
