# Keeping client data out of the tool

<!-- index: 4#12, 4#11, 4#10, 4#13 -->

Sections 01 to 04 were about rules. This section and the next are about
a firm's policy, and they rest on the CPA.com Generative AI Toolkit, a
2023 publication written for practitioners when ChatGPT was new. What it
says about how ChatGPT handles input describes the product as it was in
2023; lesson 2 gave OpenAI's current pages, and this section takes from
the toolkit its policy practices only.

## The general rule

The toolkit's general rule is one sentence: as a general rule, no client
or business information should be entered into a public LLM. Its section
on acceptable use says the same as a policy term: the policy should
prohibit uploading or asking questions about client data within a public
generative AI tool. Two things are in those sentences: the scope, client
or business information, and the object, a public tool. The scope is
wider than the Code's term. The toolkit does not say that the
information has to be confidential, only that it is the client's or the
firm's.

## "Public"

The toolkit does not define "public LLM" or "public generative AI tool".
It never names an OpenAI plan, and it does not say whether a paid
workspace is public in its sense. Lesson 2 drew its line between
OpenAI's services for individuals, which train on conversations unless
the user opts out, and its services for businesses, which do not by
default. This course reads the toolkit's public tool as including any
individual plan, whatever its training setting, so that on this course's
reading the toolkit's rule keeps client and business information out of
an individual-plan ChatGPT account; the toolkit does not draw that line,
and the course does. Whether a Business or Enterprise workspace is a
public tool in the toolkit's sense is not something the toolkit says,
and this course does not say it either; sections 02 and 04 gave the
rules that bear on it.

## Before anything is uploaded

The toolkit's sample use cases include several that upload data, and it
says of them: any examples that reference uploading data will require
all identifiable information to be removed prior to use. Its security
section makes the same practice general, for every tool: de-identify, or
sanitize, personal information before ingesting it into both internal AI
systems and public tools. Internal systems are included, and so the
practice does not depend on which side of the public line a tool is on.
The toolkit does not say what de-identifying consists of beyond its own
word, sanitize, or whether de-identified client information is still
confidential client information under the Code.

## Two practices, stated together

Together the two practices are the toolkit's answer to what goes in:
client and business information does not go into a public tool, and
personal information is de-identified before it goes into any tool.
Section 06 gives the rest of what the toolkit says a policy should
contain.
