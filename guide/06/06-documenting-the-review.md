# Documenting the review

<!-- index: 4#6, 4#5 -->

The last thing this lesson asks of a firm is a record of the review.

## What the toolkit says

In the same list in which it places the responsibility to monitor
answers, the CPA.com toolkit says: discuss with general counsel necessary
documentation of a review process for AI output. That is the whole of
what the toolkit says on the subject.

## Two things it settles

The sentence settles two things. First, the review process for AI output
is documented; the toolkit's word is *necessary*, and it does not present
the documentation as optional. Second, what the documentation must
contain is decided with general counsel, not by the person doing the
review and not by this course. What the record should contain is
therefore not something this lesson says; the toolkit sends that question
to counsel, and this lesson sends it there too.

## As firm policy

Three features of the toolkit's sentence make the record a matter of firm
policy rather than of individual habit. It is addressed to the firm,
since it is the firm that has general counsel. It concerns a process, not
a single response. And it sits beside the responsibility to monitor
answers, in the same list, as one of the ways that responsibility is
carried out. Lesson 5 is about the rest of the firm's policy, on what may
go into the tool at all; lesson 6 shows the review being done on one task
from start to finish.

## What this lesson has and has not said

It has said, on the vendor's authority, that a response is a draft and
that four kinds of content in it are always verified; on the Code's, that
a member exercises due professional care and obtains sufficient relevant
data; and on the toolkit's, that a human reviews the output and that the
review process is documented in a form counsel decides. It has not said
what the record looks like, which plans include which tools, or what
counts as a reliable source for a given engagement.
