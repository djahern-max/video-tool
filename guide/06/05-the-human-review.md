# The human review

<!-- index: 4#4, 4#5, 4#7 -->

Sections 01 to 04 said what is verified and why. This section and the
next say who does it, and how a firm makes it a matter of policy rather
than of habit.

## A human reviews

The CPA.com Generative AI Toolkit says that because generative AI output
may include biases, errors, or hallucinations, a human should review and
ensure the appropriateness and accuracy of any content that is being used
in decision-making or shared with clients. Two conditions are in that
sentence, and either one is enough on its own: content used in a
decision, and content shared with a client. The toolkit's list of risk
considerations states the responsibility in the profession's terms:
accounting professionals have a responsibility to monitor answers that
are coming out of generative AI to ensure accuracy.

## Whose responsibility

The toolkit's word is *responsibility*, and it places it on the
accounting professional, not on the vendor and not on the tool. That is
the toolkit's position, stated in a 2023 document; section 04 gave the
Code's, which does not name the tool at all. Read together, the two put
the work in the same place: on the professional.

Suppose a response gives a client the answer to a question about a filing
requirement. On the toolkit's terms it is content shared with a client,
and a person reviews it before it goes; what the person checks is the
requirement in the source, as section 02 described, and not the
response's confidence in it.

## What the review consists of

The toolkit does not say how a review is done. The vendor's list in
section 02 is the nearest thing in this lesson to a checklist: quotes,
data, technical information, and references, each checked against its
source. One practice the toolkit does describe makes that review easier
to do: prompting the model to return verbatim excerpts, so that the human
researcher gathers the passages and reaches the conclusion. Lesson 3
covered how to ask for that. Here the point is the toolkit's: the human
checks the source, and not the summary, and an excerpt with a citation is
what makes the source easy to reach.
