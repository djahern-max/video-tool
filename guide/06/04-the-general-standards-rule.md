# The General Standards Rule

Sections 01 to 03 gave the vendor's advice. This section gives the
profession's rule, in the rule's own words, and then says how this course
connects the two. The two things are kept apart, because one is the
AICPA's and the other is this course's.

## What the Rule says

The General Standards Rule of the AICPA Code of Professional Conduct, in
the Code's section 1.300, requires a member to comply with a short list
of standards. Two of them are this section's. The first is due
professional care: exercise due professional care in the performance of
professional services. The second is sufficient relevant data: obtain
sufficient relevant data to afford a reasonable basis for conclusions or
recommendations in relation to any professional services performed.
Those are the Rule's words. The Rule does not mention artificial
intelligence, a language model, or a chatbot, and nothing in this section
should be read as if it did.

## What the Code points to

Immediately under the Rule, the Code notes that a nonauthoritative
question and answer regarding the use of technology and its output is
available, in the AICPA's Ethics Questions and Answers at section 400.02,
"Using the Output of Technology". The Code does not reproduce that
guidance, and this lesson does not either. What the Code itself says is
that the question of using technology's output exists and that the AICPA
has answered it, nonauthoritatively, elsewhere.

## This course's position

What follows is the course's, not the Rule's. On OpenAI's own description
in sections 01 and 02, a response from ChatGPT is a draft that may be
incorrect, whose quotes, figures, technical content, and references the
vendor says always to verify; on this course's reading, such a response
is not, by itself, sufficient relevant data to afford a reasonable basis
for a conclusion. Verifying the response against a source is, on the
same reading, how a member who used ChatGPT obtains the data the Rule
requires: the response says where to look, and what is relied on is what
the source says. The course connects due professional care to the same
step, and it is the course, not the Rule, that says the checking is the
care.

## When someone else did part of the work

One of the Rule's interpretations, on the use of a third-party service
provider, says that the member must also obtain sufficient relevant data
to support the work product and comply with all technical standards
applicable to the professional services. Whether a model vendor is a
third-party service provider in the Code's sense is a question the Code
does not answer, and lesson 5 takes it up. What the interpretation shows
is narrower and enough for this lesson: the requirement to obtain the
data follows the member when someone else has done part of the work.
