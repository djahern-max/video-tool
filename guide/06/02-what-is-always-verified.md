# What is always verified

<!-- index: 9#9, 9#4, 9#3, 9#13, 9#12; 4#3 attributed -->

The page does not leave "important information" to the reader. Among its
practical tips is a list of four things to verify always: quotes, data,
technical information, and references to external documents. This section
takes the four in turn.

## Quotes and references

Two of the four are a form of hallucination the page names on its first
screen: fabricated quotes, studies, citations, or references to
non-existent sources. A quotation in a response is checked by finding it
in the document it is attributed to, and a reference is checked by
finding the document. If either cannot be found, the page's word for what
has happened is hallucination — a response that is not factually accurate
— and lesson 1 said what that means.

## Data

Data is the third item. An executive quoted in the CPA.com toolkit puts
the reason in one sentence: today's large language models produce
generated output, not computed answers, which means you should not trust
them with math or financial analysis. That is one executive's phrasing,
quoted in a 2023 trade-body toolkit, and lesson 1 gave OpenAI's own
version: the page ties accurate calculation to a tool it names, not to
the ordinary response. A figure in a response is therefore checked by
recomputing it or by finding it in the record it is said to come from.

## Technical information

The fourth item is the broadest, and the page gives no definition of it.
The page's other list is a clue to what it means: among the forms of
hallucination it names are incorrect definitions, dates, or facts. For a
CPA, a threshold, a rate, a filing date, or the wording of a standard is
technical information in the ordinary sense of the words, and each is
checked against the document that sets it.

## The rule, restated

The four items are a list of what not to take from a response on the
response's own authority. What "verify" means is the same in each case:
the source, not the summary. A response that quotes a standard, gives a
figure, states a rate, and cites a ruling has made four claims of the
kinds the page says always to check, and none of the four is checked by
reading the response again. Section 03 is about the case in which the
response itself points to the source.
